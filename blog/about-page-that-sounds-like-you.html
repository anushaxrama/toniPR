How to Write an About Page That Sounds Like You

May 2025 · 6 min read

A lot of About pages sound like they were written for nobody in particular. They list credentials, use broad words like “passionate” and “innovative,” and still leave a new visitor unsure what the business actually does. A good About page should feel simple, human, and easy to understand.

Start with what you do and who you help

The first job of an About page is clarity. Before the reader learns your backstory, they should understand what you do and who it is for. If that part is fuzzy, the rest of the page will not help much.

Tell the story behind the work

Once the basics are clear, give people a reason to remember you. What led you here? What problem keeps pulling you back? What do you care about that shapes how you work? You do not need a dramatic founder origin story. You just need a real explanation that sounds like a person, not a brochure.

Keep it grounded

  • Use specific language: say what you actually do instead of abstract phrases.
  • Sound like yourself: if you would not say it out loud, it probably does not belong here.
  • Make it useful: help the reader understand how you think, not just what you have done.

Make the reader feel oriented

A good About page should reduce confusion. By the end, a reader should know what kind of person or team is behind the business, what you believe, and whether you feel like the right fit for them.

Why interviews help

Many people struggle to write About pages because their best language comes out in conversation, not on command. Talking through your story often produces clearer, warmer, more believable wording than staring at a blank page.
