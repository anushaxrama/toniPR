What to Post When You Don’t Know What to Say

May 2025 · 6 min read

Most people do not struggle with content because they have nothing to say. They struggle because the moment they open a blank page, everything suddenly feels too small, too obvious, or too messy to post. The fix is not forcing yourself to become more creative on command. The fix is having a few reliable starting points.

Start with what people already ask you

The easiest content often comes from questions you answer all the time. What do clients ask before they buy? What do friends keep asking about your work? What part of your process do people misunderstand? If you answer those clearly in one post, you are already being useful.

Use simple categories instead of waiting for inspiration

  • What you believe: your point of view on your industry.
  • What you see: patterns you keep noticing with customers or clients.
  • What you learned: a lesson from a recent project, launch, or conversation.
  • What you explain: something people find confusing that you can make clearer.

Say one thing, not everything

A lot of posts feel hard because people try to pack five ideas into one piece. Pick one. One lesson, one opinion, one story, one example. The post becomes easier to write and easier to read.

Good content often starts as a conversation

If you can explain something clearly out loud, you already have content. A sentence you say in a call, a note you text a friend, or a question you answer naturally can become the seed of a post. A simple fallback prompt

If you are stuck, try this: “One thing people get wrong about my work is…” Finish that sentence and keep going for five more lines. That alone is often enough to create a strong post, article intro, or short founder note.
